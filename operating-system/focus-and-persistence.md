# Project Zero — Focus & Persistence

## Purpose

Project Zero is a long-horizon research project.

Its success depends not only on the quality of research, but on the ability to remain focused on one deliberate direction long enough to produce meaningful evidence.

This document defines the operating principles for maintaining focus, continuity, and persistence.

The objective is not to maximize working hours.

The objective is to **keep making meaningful progress without repeatedly losing the thread of the project.**

---

## 1. One Main Project

At any given period, Project Zero is the primary project.

Other ideas, projects, and opportunities may exist, but they must not continuously compete for the same cognitive attention.

This does not mean other work is forbidden.

It means:

> One project gets the primary momentum.

---

## 2. Always Have a Next Action

"Work on Project Zero" is not an actionable task.

Every working session must have a concrete next action.

Bad:

> Work on strategy evaluation.

Good:

> Define the minimum trade-count criterion in `strategy-evaluation.md`.

The next action should be small enough that starting it requires little or no decision-making.

---

## 3. Never Re-Decide the Direction During a Work Session

The beginning of a work session is not the time to reconsider the entire project.

The current direction should already be established.

During a session:

> Execute first. Reconsider direction later.

If a new idea appears, capture it and continue.

---

## 4. Parking Lot

New ideas are valuable, but they are not automatically priorities.

When a new idea appears:

1. Write it down.
2. Put it in the Parking Lot.
3. Return to the current task.

Do not immediately:

* redesign the architecture
* start a new experiment
* research a new technology
* change the methodology
* create a new project

Ideas can be reviewed later.

> Capturing an idea is enough. Acting on it is a separate decision.

---

## 5. Minimum Viable Progress

A bad day does not need to become a zero-progress day.

Define a minimum viable progress level for difficult days.

Examples:

* 20 minutes of focused work
* one experiment
* one documented observation
* one decision
* one completed small task

The purpose is continuity, not punishment.

Rest is allowed when genuinely needed.

Persistence does not mean working until exhaustion.

---

## 6. Definition of Done

Every meaningful task or milestone should have a clear completion condition.

Avoid:

> Research methodology is complete.

Prefer:

```text
[ ] Hypothesis defined
[ ] Data requirements defined
[ ] Evaluation metrics defined
[ ] Validation procedure defined
[ ] Acceptance criteria documented
```

Once the defined conditions are satisfied, the task is done.

Further improvement belongs to a new task.

---

## 7. Session Shutdown Ritual

Never end a meaningful work session without leaving a trail for the next session.

Record:

```text
DONE:
- What was completed?

LEARNED:
- What was discovered?

NEXT:
- What is the next concrete action?
```

The most important field is `NEXT`.

Tomorrow should not begin with:

> "Where was I?"

It should begin with:

> "I know exactly where to continue."

---

## 8. Progress Is Evidence, Not Hours

Time spent is not the primary measure of research progress.

Meaningful progress can be:

* an experiment completed
* a hypothesis rejected
* a misleading metric identified
* a research question clarified
* a decision documented
* a reproducible result produced

A failed hypothesis can be valuable progress.

The goal is not to prove the original idea.

The goal is to discover what is true.

---

## 9. Weekly Review

Once per week, review:

### DONE

What actually moved forward?

### LEARNED

What did we discover?

### DISTRACTIONS

What consumed attention without producing meaningful progress?

### KEEP

What should continue?

### CHANGE

What should be adjusted?

### DELETE

What should be removed?

### NEXT

What is the most important next step?

The weekly review is the appropriate place to reconsider direction.

Not every work session.

---

## 10. Commitment to Process, Not Outcome

Project Zero cannot guarantee:

* a profitable trading strategy
* a persistent market edge
* financial independence
* a particular research outcome

It can guarantee disciplined behavior:

* honest experiments
* reproducible research
* explicit hypotheses
* documented evidence
* resistance to cherry-picking
* clear validation criteria
* willingness to reject failed ideas

Therefore:

> Commit to discovering the truth, not to proving the hypothesis.

---

## 11. Project Contract

Before committing significant effort to a direction, be able to answer:

```text
Why does this project exist?

What does meaningful progress look like?

What is the current hypothesis?

What evidence would support it?

What evidence would reject it?

What are we explicitly NOT going to do?

Under what conditions should this direction be abandoned?

What is the current NEXT ACTION?
```

---

## 12. The Core Rule

> **Never allow the size of the project to determine the size of today's task.**

The project may take years.

Today's task may take 25 minutes.

That is enough.

---

## Operating Principle

Project Zero should continuously move through this loop:

```text
CURRENT DIRECTION
       ↓
NEXT ACTION
       ↓
FOCUSED WORK
       ↓
RESULT / EVIDENCE
       ↓
DOCUMENT
       ↓
NEXT ACTION
       ↓
REPEAT
```

The purpose of this system is simple:

> **Keep the project moving without allowing complexity, novelty, or temporary difficulty to repeatedly pull it off course.**
