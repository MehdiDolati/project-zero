# Purpose

Governance defines how Project Zero preserves consistency, quality, and long-term integrity.

Its purpose is not controlling contributors.

Its purpose is protecting the research process.

---

# Definition

Governance is the collection of principles, policies, rules, constraints, and review mechanisms that guide how knowledge is created, modified, validated, and preserved.

---

# Layers of Governance

Project Zero distinguishes several governance layers.

## Principles

Timeless beliefs that define the philosophy of the project.

Example:

- Repository is the Source of Truth.

---

## Policies

High-level operational intentions.

Policies describe what should happen.

Example:

Research must be reproducible.

---

## Rules

Concrete requirements that can be verified.

Example:

Every Hypothesis MUST define falsification criteria.

---

## Constraints

Non-negotiable boundaries.

Example:

No custom software before validated methodology.

---

## Recommendations

Good practices that improve quality but are not mandatory.

---

## Enforcement

Governance may be enforced through:

- human review,
- checklists,
- automated validation,
- repository tooling.

Automation should strengthen governance rather than define it.

---

# Evolution

Governance is expected to evolve.

Every governance change should itself follow the project's research methodology.

Governance is not exempt from evidence.

---

# Philosophy

Good governance reduces cognitive load.

It should eliminate unnecessary decisions while preserving intellectual freedom where exploration is valuable.

Rules should exist only where consistency creates value.

Everything else should remain flexible.
