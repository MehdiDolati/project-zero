# Research Methodology

## Purpose

The purpose of this methodology is to maximize learning while minimizing false discoveries.

The objective is not to prove ideas correct.

The objective is to continuously improve decision quality through disciplined experimentation and evidence-based reasoning.

---

# Core Principle

Project Zero is a research operation.

Software, AI, automation, and trading strategies are outcomes of research—not the research itself.

---

# Research Loop

Every research activity should ultimately contribute to the following continuous learning cycle:

Question

↓

Hypothesis or Observation

↓

Experiment

↓

Evidence

↓

Evaluation

↓

Decision

↓

Knowledge Update

↓

New Question

---

# Atomic Unit of Learning

The atomic unit of learning is the **Experiment**.

An experiment is a repeatable process designed to generate evidence that supports or challenges a hypothesis or investigates an unexpected observation.

No claim should become project knowledge without experimental evidence.

---

# Two Discovery Paths

Project Zero recognizes two valid ways of discovering new ideas.

## 1. Hypothesis-Driven Research

Research begins with an explicit question or hypothesis.

Example:

- formulate a hypothesis
- design an experiment
- collect evidence
- evaluate the results

This should be the default research approach.

---

## 2. Serendipity-Driven Research

Unexpected observations are valuable.

Interesting patterns discovered during research should never be ignored simply because they were not anticipated.

However, unexpected discoveries must follow the same validation process as any other idea.

Observation is not evidence.

Evidence is not knowledge.

---

# Knowledge Maturity

Information becomes progressively more valuable as confidence increases.

Observation

↓

Evidence

↓

Claim

↓

Knowledge

Definitions:

## Observation

Something noticed during research.

No conclusion should be drawn from observation alone.

---

## Evidence

Results produced by reproducible experiments.

Evidence increases or decreases confidence in a claim.

---

## Claim

A statement supported by evidence but still open to challenge.

Claims remain provisional.

---

## Knowledge

A claim that has survived repeated evaluation and is sufficiently trusted to influence future decisions.

Knowledge is always revisable.

---

# Decision Making

Every important decision should explicitly reference:

- supporting evidence
- challenged assumptions
- competing explanations
- confidence level

Decisions without traceable reasoning should be avoided.

---

# Failure

Negative results are valuable.

Rejecting a weak idea is considered successful research.

Failed experiments should be preserved because they reduce repeated mistakes and improve future learning.

---

# Role of Automation

Automation is introduced only after a process demonstrates value through repeated manual execution.

Software should amplify validated methodology.

It must never replace critical thinking.

---

# Research Quality Criteria

A research process is considered healthy when it:

- produces reproducible experiments,
- preserves reasoning,
- encourages criticism,
- minimizes confirmation bias,
- detects false discoveries,
- captures unexpected findings,
- continuously improves decision quality.

---

# Guiding Principle

Research exists to reduce uncertainty—not eliminate it.

The goal is not certainty.

The goal is making better decisions under uncertainty.

---

# Open Questions

The methodology itself is subject to continuous improvement.

Every part of this process may be revised if evidence demonstrates a better approach.
